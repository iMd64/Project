WEEK 1

Introduction to Project Management

Lesson 1 • Characteristics, Volume vs. Variety, Cross-Functional Boundary & Five Generations

Slide 12 • Figure 1.1 Seven Core Characteristics

Project characteristics and their management implications (Figure 1.1)

Key Exam Takeaways:

• Emergence and uncertainty: Detailed requirements may not be known initially; PMs must lead through ambiguity.

• Aspects of uniqueness: The exact project has not been done before, though parts may have precedents.

• Temporary: Definite start and end dates; teams dissolve and funding ceases at project completion.

• Social construction: Involves human beings and stakeholder networks, not mechanical systems.

Slide 14 • Figure 1.2 Volume vs. Variety Continuum

Volume vs. variety and three types of project (Figure 1.2)

1. 'Painting by numbers'

Well-known process & outcome (e.g., modular franchise construction). Highest volume, lowest uncertainty.

2. 'As . . . but . . .'

Resembles previous projects with amendments (e.g., new engine variant for an existing airframe).

3. 'First-timers'

High novelty in both process and outcome (e.g., first Mars rover landing). Maximum uncertainty and variety.

Slide 26 • Figure 2.6 IDeA Foundation Armenia Example

Stage gates: assess before continuing (Figure 2.6)

Figure 2.6 Authentic Lifecycle Model
Lifecycle & Stage-Gate Mechanics: • Pre-Project vs. Project: Capture Idea is the pre-project incubation phase. The formal project spans Gates 0 to 5 (from Initiate Project through Execute), handing over to ongoing Operations at Gate 5.
• Decision Gates at Boundaries: Gates sit directly on the transitions between phases to evaluate continuing strategic, funding, delivery, financing, execution, and operational viability.
• Continuous Rules (Gates 0–3): The Opportunity Statement is iteratively refined at each gate, and time, cost, and resource estimates are formally committed before unlocking subsequent phases.

Slides 18 & 22 • Figures 4.4 & 4.5 Constraints & Trade-Offs

The Iron Triangle & Objectives Trade-off Matrix (GTO)

Figure 4.4 The Iron Triangle
GTO Classification (Givens, Tradeables, Optimisers)
G = Givens: Non-negotiable fixed boundaries (e.g., hard regulatory deadlines, safety laws).
T = Tradeables: Flexible parameters adjusted to protect the core givens.
O = Optimisers: Dimensions where added effort maximizes value if exceeded.
Zone of No Brain Warning: Operating strictly in the center triangle without negotiating trade-offs assumes Time, Cost, and Quality can all be fixed simultaneously with zero tolerance—a frequent driver of project failure.

Lesson 6 • WBS/OBS/PBS Trees, Activity Box, CPM Network Passes, Critical Path & Gantt Grid

Slide 03 Core Distinction: Plan vs. Schedule vs. Gantt Chart

A time plan is an analysis tool

📖 Exact Book Quote

"Having prepared a plan (not date dependent), the next stage is to compile the schedule (e.g. we will start on this date). The Gantt chart is just one way to display this all-important schedule."

🎓 Teaching Note First establish what work is needed, its sequence and estimated duration. Then apply start dates, calendars and available resources to make a schedule. A Gantt chart displays that schedule.
Visualizing the 3-Stage Progression (Exam Essential)
STAGE 1: TIME PLAN

Not Date Dependent

  • What work is needed (WBS)
  • Sequence / logic (Network)
  • Estimated duration (e.g., Day 0 to Day 24)
STAGE 2: SCHEDULE

Date & Resource Dependent

  • Apply specific start dates ("start on this date")
  • Apply working calendars (weekends/holidays)
  • Apply available resources & capacity
STAGE 3: GANTT CHART

Display Tool

  • Just one visual way to display the schedule
  • Places activity bars on a horizontal time axis
  • For others to read, challenge, and use

Four Ways to Link Two Activities & The Activity Box

Figure 6.6 Four Activity Dependencies (FS, SS, FF, SF)
Finish-to-Start (FS) Finish A → Start B (Most Common)
Start-to-Start (SS) Start A → Start B
Finish-to-Finish (FF) Finish A → Finish B
Start-to-Finish (SF) Start A → Finish B (Rare)
Figure 6.10 The Six-Number Activity Box
Box Standard Convention: • Top Row: Earliest Start (EST) | Duration (Dur) | Earliest Finish (EFT = EST + Dur)
• Middle Row: Activity Identifier / Description Label
• Bottom Row: Latest Start (LST = LFT − Dur) | Total Float (Float = LS − ES = LF − EF) | Latest Finish (LFT)
Slide 16 • Figure 6.5 Task dependency

A simple dependency: 6 + 7 = 13 days

🔍 How to Read It Activity B cannot start until A finishes. With A = 6 days and B = 7 days, the earliest completion is day 13. This is a finish-to-start dependency, with day 0 as the starting point.
Slide 18 • Figure 6.7 Multiple dependencies (1)

One successor may have two prerequisites

ES(C) = max[ EF(A), EF(B) ]
🔍 How to Read It C waits for both A and B. Its earliest start is the later of their earliest finish times: ES(C) = max[EF(A), EF(B)]. A delay in either predecessor matters only if it makes that predecessor the latest to finish.
Slide 19 • Figure 6.8 Multiple dependencies (2)

Branches must rejoin before shared work

🔍 How to Read It The final activity H waits for E, F and G. Parallel work may reduce overall duration, but the latest finishing predecessor controls H's start. List every true dependency; do not link unrelated tasks for convenience.
